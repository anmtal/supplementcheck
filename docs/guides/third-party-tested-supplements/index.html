Third-Party Tested Supplements: What NSF, USP & Informed Sport Mean

Dietary supplements are not approved by the FDA before they're sold — the agency mostly acts after something goes wrong. Third-party certification is the closest thing to independent proof that what's on the label is what's in the bottle, and that it's free of dangerous contaminants.

What each seal actually verifies

  • USP Verified — confirms identity, potency, and freedom from harmful levels of contaminants, made under good manufacturing practices.
  • NSF Certified — tests that label claims match contents and screens for contaminants. NSF Certified for Sport adds screening for 280+ substances banned in sport.
  • Informed Sport / Informed Choice — batch-level testing for banned substances, popular with athletes.

How to check a product

Find the seal on the label, then confirm it in the certifier's public online directory (USP, NSF and Informed Sport all publish searchable lists). A seal printed on the label but missing from the directory is a red flag.

Why it matters

Independent testing repeatedly finds supplements with undeclared drugs, wrong doses, or heavy-metal contamination. Certification won't prove a supplement works, but it dramatically lowers the odds of a nasty surprise.

Frequently asked

Does third-party tested mean FDA approved?
No. The FDA does not approve supplements before sale. Third-party certification is independent testing by a private organization (USP, NSF, Informed Sport) and is separate from any government approval.
Is NSF or USP better?
Both are well respected and test identity, potency and contaminants. NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Sport add banned-substance screening for athletes. Any of them beats an uncertified product.
How can I verify a certification is real?
Search the certifier's official online directory for the exact product and manufacturer. If it isn't listed, treat the on-label seal with suspicion.
