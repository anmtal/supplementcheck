Banned Supplement Ingredients: The FDA List

The FDA can't approve supplements in advance, but it can ban ingredients once they're shown to be dangerous — and it regularly catches products spiked with drugs that were never declared on the label. Here are the big ones.

Ingredients the FDA has banned or acted against

  • Ephedra (ephedrine alkaloids) — banned in supplements in 2004 after deaths linked to weight-loss and energy products.
  • DMAA and DMHA — stimulants the FDA says are illegal in supplements; common in pre-workout and weight-loss products.
  • Sibutramine — a prescription weight-loss drug pulled from the US market in 2010, still found hidden in "natural" diet pills.
  • Sildenafil and tadalafil — the active drugs in Viagra and Cialis, repeatedly found undeclared in "male enhancement" supplements.
  • Phenolphthalein — a suspected carcinogen found hidden in some weight-loss products.
  • BMPEA, picamilon, higenamine, SARMs — synthetic or pharmaceutical compounds the FDA has flagged as not legitimate dietary ingredients.

How to check your supplement

Most of these turn up in weight-loss, sexual-enhancement and bodybuilding products. Search our database for your product, or browse the recalls below where the FDA found a hidden drug.

Frequently asked

How do I know if a supplement contains a banned ingredient?
Check the FDA's tainted-products database, which we mirror here, for your product or brand. Banned stimulants and hidden drugs are most common in weight-loss, sexual-enhancement and muscle-building products.
Is DMAA illegal?
The FDA considers DMAA an illegal ingredient in dietary supplements and has issued warning letters and seizures, though it still appears in some pre-workout and weight-loss products sold online.
Are these ingredients always on the label?
Often not. Many are added secretly, which is exactly why the FDA's tainted-products list exists: the drug was found by lab testing, not declared on the label.
